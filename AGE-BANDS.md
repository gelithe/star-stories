# Age bands — the craft of translation

This is the intellectual core of Star Stories. Read it before writing any book.
The product is not "a horoscope for kids." It is **one chart truth, translated
into the register a specific age can receive.** Get this wrong and the books
become either generic or creepy. Get it right and they feel inevitable.

---

## The one non-negotiable principle

**Story as mirror, never prediction as destiny.**

The chart gives the story its *shape* — never its *verdict*. We say "this is a
story about the kind of heart you were born with," never "you will be…". No
careers foretold, no relationships fated, no fixed character sentence a child
must carry. A child inherits a **poem, not a box.**

Practical test for any line: could it make a child feel *trapped* by who they
are, or *seen* in who they are? Keep only the second.

---

## The translation method (how a placement becomes a story)

Every book is built the same way, regardless of age:

1. **Read the chart for the ONE dominant truth.** Not ten placements — the
   single structural fact that most defines this child. (Nova: a Scorpio Moon
   that feels everything. Lars: a serious surface over a Pisces ocean. Luis:
   fire that only becomes itself with others.)
2. **Name the gift and its shadow together.** Deep feeling *and* overwhelm.
   Serious calm *and* hidden flood. Drive *and* the lonely solo run.
3. **Find the metaphor a child can hold.** Feelings → a sea / a wave / a weather.
   Not "Scorpio Moon" — "a moon that rocks her when the feelings get big."
4. **Choose the register for the age** (below). Same truth, different telling.
5. **End with the practical gift** — the one repeatable thing that helps
   (name the feeling; wait for the wave; let the team in). This is the sentence
   the parents will thank you for.

The age band only changes **step 4**. Steps 1–3 and 5 are constant. That is why
the same child can be re-given the book at every age: it is the same truth,
growing up alongside them.

## The bands

| Band | Reader does | Cognitive/emotional frame | Form | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0–2** | Listens, looks | Rhythm, sound, faces; no plot | Lullaby: image + one line, repeated | 6–8 spreads |
| **2–5** | Bedtime with adult | Simple cause→effect; one feeling at a time; loves repetition | Animal/gentle fable, a recurring chant | 7–9 spreads |
| **6–8** | Early self-reading | Hero + problem + change; can hold a small arc | Quest with a companion who "evolves" | 5–7 short chapters |
| **9–12** | Private reading | Interiority begins; wants to be taken seriously | Adventure + first inner life; a secret strength | 8–12 pages, chapters |
| **13–17** | Skeptical reading | Identity, intensity, allergy to being patronized | Honest, spare, unpatronizing; questions over answers | short, potent |
| **Young adult** | Leaving home | Meaning, direction, self-authorship | A letter to carry — the deep portrait, addressed to them | essay/letter |
| **Parents' companion** | The adults | Wants the real chart + the "why" | Reflective; placement → story mapping | 1–2 pages per book |

## The same truth across every band (worked example)

Take a child with a **strong, deep-feeling Moon** (Nova's real Scorpio Moon:
feels everything, all the way down; needs feelings *held*, not fixed).

- **0–2:** *"When the feelings are big, the moon rocks her."* (One image. The
  child hears safety, not a lesson.)
- **2–5:** A little bear whose feelings come like weather. When the storm comes,
  Mama Bear doesn't chase it away — she sits in the cave until it passes.
  *Chant: "Storms pass. I am still here."*
- **6–8:** A girl who discovers her feelings are a deep sea with treasure at the
  bottom — but only if she dives instead of fearing it. Her companion glows
  brighter each time she names what she feels.
- **9–12:** She learns that feeling deeply is not a weakness others must manage —
  it's a kind of sight. She can sense the weather in a room. The secret: she
  doesn't have to *act* on every wave, only *notice* it.
- **13–17:** Honest and direct: your intensity is real and it isn't too much.
  The task isn't to turn it down — it's to learn which feelings are weather
  (passing) and which are signal (worth acting on).
- **Young adult:** A letter: your depth will always be the first thing and the
  hardest thing. Here is how it has protected you, and here is the one way it
  has cost you.
- **Parents:** *"A Scorpio Moon feels everything, all the way down. She doesn't
  need her feelings fixed; she needs them held."* + the placement.

One truth. Seven tellings. That is the whole company.

## Worked band examples (from the real books)

These three exist, fully written, in `books/`. Study them as the reference
implementations.

### 2–5 — Luis, *The Magic Pass* (`books/luis-5.html`)
- **Chart truth:** Aries Sun in the 7th house — fire that only becomes itself
  *with others*; plus Emotional authority (feelings arrive in waves).
- **Register moves:** one feeling per scene; a physical problem (wants to score
  alone, ball rolls away); a wise animal (the cat) delivers the gift in one line
  — *"wait for your wave"*; a recurring chant in all three languages
  (*Perduok! Passa! Pass!*).
- **Gift:** the joy doubles when shared; wait for the wave before you act.

### 6–8 — Lars, *The Ocean of Dreams* (`books/lars-7.html`)
- **Chart truth:** Capricorn rising (serious, solid) over a Pisces 1st-house
  stellium (an inner ocean of feeling). "A little mountain with an ocean inside."
- **Register moves:** a real arc across 5 chapters; a companion (Aquari) who
  *evolves* each time Lars does the brave thing; the antagonist is not a monster
  but **unnamed feelings** ("fog"); the sword that cuts nothing (some things are
  dissolved, not defeated); the crew (siblings + cat) arrives to help.
- **Gift:** naming a feeling is the only magic that works on the fog.

### 0–2 / crossover — Nova, *Little Star* (`books/nova-2.html`)
- **Chart truth:** Pisces rising ("from the sea of stars"), Cancer Sun (home is
  her sun), Scorpio Moon (held by the moon).
- **Register moves:** one line per spread, one image; lullaby cadence; brothers
  as "two bright stars"; ends on *"sleep, little star, the sky holds you."*
- **Gift (for the parent, not the baby):** feelings held, not fixed.

## Not-yet-written bands (sketches to build from)

### 9–12 — the "secret compass" register
Interiority is dawning. The child wants to be trusted with something real. The
chart trait becomes a **private strength only they carry** — not shown off, but
known. Structure: a realistic protagonist (lightly fictionalized as themselves)
facing an ordinary but real challenge; the placement is the thing that, once
they stop hiding it, becomes the way through. First-person diary or close third
person works well. Honesty over whimsy; they can smell a talking-down.

### 13–17 — the honest register
No fairy frame. Direct address, short, spare, a little uncomfortable in the way
true things are (borrow the *tone* of the Chart Compass "Story" portrait, dialed
to their age). Lead with what they suspect about themselves and confirm it is
real. Give tensions to hold, not traits to be. One or two true phrases in the
family languages; never a full trilingual sing-song (that reads as childish to
them). Questions land harder than answers.

### Young adult — the letter
Essentially the Chart Compass deep portrait, re-addressed as a letter for
someone stepping into their own life. Longer, reflective, keepsake. This is the
"leaving home" gift — a thing to put in a drawer and reread at 25.

### Parents' companion — always included
Every book ends with it. Map each story beat back to the actual placement, name
the one practical gift, and restate the ethic: *a story is a mirror, not a map
of destiny.* Language selectable (currently Italian for the three books).

## Craft rules that hold across all bands

- **One truth per book.** Resist cramming the chart. Depth beats coverage.
- **Metaphor before terminology.** A child never meets "Scorpio Moon" — only the
  moon that rocks her.
- **Gift, not label.** End on something repeatable and kind, never a verdict.
- **Family words stay family words** (names, pets — Titty is Titty in every
  language). See `LANGUAGES.md` for the mixing shapes per age.
- **Siblings and real details are gold** — they turn a personalized book into
  *their* book. Ask for one or two true things (a pet, a love, a sibling).
- **Read it aloud before you trust it.** The real QA is a child's attention span
  and a parent's catch in the throat.
